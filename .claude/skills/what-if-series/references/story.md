# The long story

The seven beats in `what-if-animation/references/formula.md` are the shape of a full episode. A series stretches them
across parts, and each part still has to work alone: someone may watch only that one as a short.

## How the beats spread

Proportions, for any number of parts:

| Share | Beat | What happens | In the first series (15 parts) |
|---|---|---|---|
| First part | 1 Cold open and title | Three demos, the title card, a turn line | 1 |
| 5-10% | 2 Premise and rules | How it began, the rule, who it doesn't touch | 2 |
| 10-20% | 3 The fun part | Escalating wins, small to world-sized | 3-4 |
| 25-30% | 4 The cost | Real numbers; one thing breaks after another; it turns personal | 5-8 |
| 10-15% | Escape | Something forces them to run | 9 |
| 20-25% | 5-6 Someone powerful, a choice | The suit, the threat to family, the idea said aloud | 10-12 |
| 10-15% | The price | The way out costs something real | 13-14 |
| Last part | 7 Ending | A quiet coda, then a question for the comments | 15 |

For more parts, add demos to beat 3, more real costs to beat 4, and setbacks before the choice, rather than padding.

## What each part needs

- **45 to 65 words**, second person, present tense, three to eight words a sentence. Every line a picture. At this
  length Spanish ran 18 to 22 s a part after tightening.
- **Opens clean:** the first words place you ("Día tres.", "Te llevan a un búnker.").
- **Ends on a hook:** a threat, a question or a reversal. Never a resolution, until the last part.
- **Title card in part 1 only.** Later parts use a small readout such as a day chip. Each part has no intro or recap.

## What makes it hold for minutes

- **Something planted early pays off late.** The wish in part 2 gives the way out in part 13. The sister's "idiota" in
  part 2 is the last line of the film. Plan these before writing.
- **One person who is outside the power,** so there's one honest voice and something real at stake.
- **One sentence the whole thing is about,** said only once, near the choice ("Amor es poder decir que no").
- **One power word, sound and look,** used until it has taught the viewer, then dropped when the story darkens.
- **Mood falls with the story:** bright dusk, then red sky, then concrete and night. Music follows.

## Facts

A number in the script is worked out first and recorded in that part's `science` field: the claim, the working, the
source. Add "That's real" only when it's solid. If a figure is contested or rests on your own assumption, attribute it
in the line ("Según Dunbar...") and say so in the video's description. Read the primary source if you can; if you only
reached a summary, tell the person that at handover.
